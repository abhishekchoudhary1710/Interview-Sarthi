“What are your salary expectations?”: how to answer without losing money

The one interview question with a direct rupee value on the answer. Research a range, anchor it, deliver it calmly, and retire “as per company norms” forever.

Updated 1 September 2026 · 6 min read

Every other interview answer earns you the offer. This one prices it. And yet the most common answer in India is still “as per company norms”, a sentence that reliably costs candidates a lakh or more, because it hands the number to the side whose job is to keep it low.

Why “as per company norms” fails

Companies don't have a “norm”; they have a budget band for the role: a bottom and a top, often 30–50% apart. Someone in the process knows that band precisely, and you don't. When you decline to name a number, you are not being polite; you are volunteering for the bottom of the band. The band doesn't move for politeness. It moves for a researched range stated without drama.

Step 1: research the range before the call

  • Look up the specific role at the specific company (and city) on the salary data sites: AmbitionBox, Glassdoor, Levels.fyi for tech. Company-specific numbers beat generic “average salary for X” articles every time.
  • Ask one human. A senior from college inside the company, or anyone who interviewed there recently, will calibrate you better than any website.
  • Set your range so the bottom is a number you'd genuinely be happy with; negotiations gravitate to the bottom of whatever range you state. Anchor the top slightly above your target.

Step 2: the script

English

“My current fixed is 8 LPA. For this role, based on the market and what I bring, I'm looking at the 12 to 14 range, and within that range I'm flexible if the role and growth are right. May I ask what band you've budgeted for the position?”

Four moves in four sentences: current stated without apology, a researched range, flexibility inside the range (not below it), and the question returned. Asking their band is normal, and sometimes they answer.

Hinglish (if the conversation is running in Hinglish; numbers stay in English)

“Current CTC 8 LPA hai, fixed. Market range is role ke liye main 12 se 14 dekh raha hoon, aur us range mein main flexible hoon agar role aur growth sahi ho. Aapka budget kya hai is position ke liye?”

Same structure. Why numbers stay in English, and everything else about code-mixing under pressure, is in the Hinglish answer guide.

The variations that trip people up

“What's your current CTC?” asked first

In India you'll usually be asked, and stonewalling stalls processes. The practical play: answer it, then immediately reframe to the market: “Current is 8 fixed. But I'd rather anchor on what this role is worth. Market range main 12–14 dekh raha hoon.” If your current salary is low, that reframe is everything: the market rate for the role is your basis, not a percentage on top of an underpaid number. The gap between your CTC and the market is the reason you're switching, own it instead of apologising for it.

The fresher version

At mass recruiters (TCS, Infosys, Wipro) fresher packages are fixed slabs; negotiating one signals you won't join, and the right answer is simply that you're aware of the package and fine with it (more on that round in the TCS/Infosys/Wipro HR guide). At startups and product companies, freshers can state a range: research the company's actual fresher offers first, and keep the range modest but named.

“That's above our budget”

Not a rejection, an invitation. Two honest responses: ask what the band is and decide against your real bottom number, or trade: “If 12 isn't possible on fixed, main joining bonus ya 6-month review pe baat kar sakta hoon.” A six-month review commitment in writing is often easier for them to give than base salary, and worth real money if you deliver.

The counter-offer trap at your current company

If your resignation triggers a counter-offer, remember why you interviewed at all: the reasons (scope, growth, team) rarely change with the number. Take the counter into account, but statistics and every HR forum agree: most people who accept counter-offers are searching again within a year.

Delivery beats content

The script above only works said calmly, at normal speed, with a full stop at the end. Rushed, mumbled, or trailed off (“12 to 14… but I mean, I'm flexible, whatever works…”) it reads as a bluff and gets treated like one. Practise saying your range out loud until it sounds boring. Boring is what confidence sounds like in a salary conversation. And if you tend to blank under pressure in general, the fixes in stop blanking out in online interviews apply doubly to this question; it usually lands at the end of the call, when you're most tired.
