TCS, Infosys & Wipro HR round: the questions that actually come up

The HR round at India's mass recruiters is not a personality test — it's a consistency and logistics check. Here is what they ask, why they ask it, and the answers that clear it.

Updated 1 September 2026 · 7 min read

By the time you reach the HR round at TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Capgemini or Cognizant, the technical bar is behind you. What is left is a different kind of interview, and preparing for it like a technical round — memorising clever answers — misses what is actually being checked:

  • Consistency. Do your dates, marks, gaps and documents match what you typed into the application portal months ago? HR has that form open while you talk.
  • Logistics. Will you relocate anywhere, work shifts, sign the service agreement — without drama at allocation time?
  • Joining risk. Are you likely to accept, join, and stay past training, or are you holding this offer while you interview elsewhere?

The single most important rule: your answers must match your application form. The portal answers you gave at registration — willing to relocate, no gaps, backlog count — are on the screen in front of the interviewer. Most HR rejections at mass recruiters are not bad answers; they are answers that contradict the form.

The questions, and how to answer them

1. “Are you willing to relocate?” / “Location preference?”

If you are genuinely flexible: “Yes — I've thought about it, and I'm fine with any base location. I do have a preference for Pune because my brother is there, but it's a preference, not a condition.” A stated preference is fine; a hidden condition is not. If you have one hard constraint, say it now, once, with the reason — discovering it at allocation is what burns bridges. Never tick “anywhere” in the form and then negotiate in the interview; that mismatch is the classic avoidable rejection.

2. “Are you comfortable with shifts?”

Support and infrastructure roles genuinely run 24×7, so this is a real question, not a trap. If yes, say yes plainly. If you cannot do permanent night shifts, the honest middle: “Rotational shifts are fine. If a role is permanent night shift, I'd want to know at allocation — main manage kar lunga short term, lekin long term mera performance day roles mein better hoga.” Notice the shape: flexibility first, constraint second, no drama.

3. “You know about the service agreement?”

TCS and several others ask freshers to sign a service agreement (typically one to two years, with an amount payable if you leave early — check the current terms for your batch, they change). The question is checking that you know and accept it. The answer is two sentences: “Yes, I've read it — a service commitment with an amount if I leave early. I'm planning to stay and grow here, so I'm comfortable signing it.” Do not open a negotiation about it; freshers' terms are fixed.

4. “Explain the gap / the backlogs / the low percentage.”

Fact, one-line reason, recovery, stop: “Second year mein do backlogs the — main us saal overconfident tha, honestly. Dono next semester mein clear kiye, aur final year CGPA 8.1 tha.” The recovery number is the answer; the reason is just context. Over-explaining reads as panic, and panic is what they are actually screening for. The full pattern for gap answers is in our 10 HR questions guide.

5. “Why TCS / Infosys / Wipro?”

Everyone says “brand name and growth”. One level more specific is enough to stand out: name the thing that is actually true for you — “Two reasons. The training — your ILP/foundation program is structured in a way a startup can't offer a fresher. And project variety: I'd rather see three domains in my first two years than lock into one product before I know what I like.” Both are real advantages of a services company, so it doesn't sound like flattery.

6. “Any other offers? Higher package offers?”

Honesty, without leverage games: “Ek aur process chal raha hai, but this is my preference — main yahan ke liye serious hoon.” They are estimating joining probability, not opening a negotiation — fresher packages at mass recruiters are fixed slabs, and trying to negotiate one is a red flag, not a move. (For lateral/experienced hiring the salary conversation is real — that scenario is covered in the salary expectations guide.)

7. “Tell me about yourself”

Still the opener, even here. Thirty seconds, present → proof → next — the full structure with fresher templates is in the self-introduction guide.

What actually gets people rejected in this round

  • Contradicting the application form — the number one cause, and fully avoidable. Re-read your portal entries the night before.
  • Visible unwillingness on relocation/shifts after ticking yes in the form.
  • Badmouthing a previous employer, internship, or your own college. Zero tolerance for this everywhere, but especially in a rubric-scored round.
  • Negotiating a fixed fresher package. It signals you'll decline the offer anyway.
  • Document mess — missing marksheets or ID mismatches. Not an interview failure, but it stalls offers for weeks. Have everything scanned before the round.

Note what is not on that list: imperfect English. These rounds are bilingual in practice — if the interviewer relaxes into Hindi, answer in natural Hinglish and keep the numbers in English. How to do that without sounding casual: the Hinglish answer guide.
