Accenture, Cognizant & Capgemini HR round: what they actually ask

The second family of India's mass recruiters runs its HR round with one extra, unwritten test the TCS-style round doesn't: your answers are the communication assessment. Here's what recurs, and how to answer it.

Updated 1 September 2026 · 7 min read

We've covered TCS, Infosys and Wipro separately, and the fundamentals carry over completely: match your application form, be genuinely flexible or honestly specific, never badmouth, have your documents scanned. If you haven't read that guide, start there — this one covers what's different about Accenture, Cognizant and Capgemini, and the questions that recur in their rounds specifically. (Exact process names and rounds change batch to batch at all three; the questions below are the stable part.)

The unwritten test at these three: communication is being scored the whole time. A large share of their roles are client-facing — you may be on calls with a US or UK client within your first year. So the HR/managerial round doubles as a communication sample. The fix is not an accent; it's structure. Answer in organised units — “Two reasons. One… Two…” — finish your sentences, and stop when the answer is done. Structured beats fluent, every time.

The questions that recur

1. “Tell me about yourself” — scored harder here

Because it's the communication sample in miniature. Thirty seconds, present → proof → next, one number in the middle. The full structure with fresher templates is in the self-introduction guide — for these companies, practise it out loud until it lands clean, because how you say it is half the score.

2. “Are you flexible with shifts and location?”

All three run global-client support and delivery work, so this is asked with intent. Same rule as always: match your form, be honest about hard constraints now rather than at allocation. One workable shape: “Rotational shifts theek hain. Permanent nights would affect my long-term performance, so I'd flag that at allocation — but I'm not putting conditions on joining.”

3. “What do you know about the company?”

Asked more here than anywhere. The bar is embarrassingly low — most candidates say “it's a big MNC” — so one specific sentence clears it: what the company actually does (consulting + technology services), one recent thing you read about it, and why that matters to your role. Five minutes on the company's newsroom page the night before is the entire preparation.

4. “Explain your gap / backlogs.”

Fact, one-line reason, recovery, stop. The pattern is in the TCS guide, and the deep version — for long gaps, exam-prep years, layoffs — is in the career-gap guide. What these companies specifically check: that the gap story you tell matches the dates on your documents, because background verification at all three is strict and post-offer.

5. “Will you pursue higher studies / government exams?”

The honest-but-safe answer: your current focus is this role and growing in it. Do not announce an exit plan in the joining interview — “I'll try for an MBA next year” is the most common self-inflicted rejection in these rounds. If further study is a genuine plan years away, it does not need to be volunteered today.

6. “Why Accenture / Cognizant / Capgemini?” — and why not the other two

One level above “brand name” is enough: “Two reasons — the training structure for freshers, and project variety: I'd rather see three domains in my first two years than lock into one product. And specifically here, the [consulting exposure / client mix / stream you were hired into] fits what I want to build.” Swap the bracket for the company in front of you.

7. For experienced hires: “current CTC, expected CTC, notice period”

Unlike fresher slabs, lateral hiring at these three is a real negotiation. State current plainly, give a researched range, return the question — the full playbook including the “above our budget” comeback is in the salary guide. On notice period: 90 days is standard at Indian services companies and everyone knows it; say whether buyout is an option and leave it there.

What gets people rejected here specifically

  • Unstructured rambling. The communication test again — a correct answer delivered as a five-minute wander scores worse than a modest answer in three clean sentences.
  • Contradicting the application form — same as everywhere, still the number one cause.
  • Announcing exit plans (studies, exams, startup dreams) in the joining interview.
  • Document-story mismatch. All three run post-offer background verification; dates and salary figures you state must match what your documents will show.
  • Treating the round as won. It mostly is — but “mostly” is exactly the margin the five mistakes above eat.
