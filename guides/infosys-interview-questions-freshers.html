Infosys interview questions for freshers: one round, everything in it

Infosys compresses what other companies spread across three rounds into one sitting: technical, behavioural and HR questions arrive in the same 30–40 minutes. That changes how you prepare. Here is the process, the money, and the questions.

Updated 2 September 2026 · 8 min read

The one-round trap: because there's no separate HR round, candidates walk in prepared for coding and get blindsided by “are you open to relocation?” forty seconds after a SQL question. Prepare the HR logistics answers to the same standard as your technical answers. They carry the same weight here.

Clearing its exam has meant direct interview chances and DSE consideration in recent cycles. If it's on your resume, revise its syllabus: interviewers treat a listed certification as fair game.

The technical questions Infosys actually asks

  • Your project, then one level deeper. Same opening as everywhere. But Infosys panels reliably follow with “what would you change if you rebuilt it today?” Prepare a real answer; it separates candidates who built the project from those who watched it get built.
  • OOP, thoroughly. The four pillars with examples from your own project, not textbook definitions. Abstraction vs encapsulation trips more freshers than any coding question.
  • SQL and DBMS. Joins, normalisation up to 3NF in plain words, primary vs unique key. If your resume says a database, expect a query written live.
  • A short program, watched live: palindrome, Fibonacci, count vowels, reverse a string without built-ins. They're watching your thinking-out-loud, not your syntax memory.
  • Software engineering vocabulary: SDLC stages, what Agile means in one sentence, what testing you did on your project. Services interviews love process questions because delivery is process.

The HR questions, Infosys edition

The generic scripts, gaps, backlogs, other offers, are in the mass-recruiter HR guide. What is specifically Infosys about this part:

  • “Are you ready for the training program?” This is a real question about a real thing: months of structured, residential foundation training with assessments. And repeatedly failing those assessments can cost trainees the job. The winning answer is an informed yes: “Yes, honestly, Mysuru campus wala training is one of the reasons Infosys is high on my list. Structured start milta hai jo self-learning se nahi milta.” Not knowing about it reads as zero research.
  • “Why Infosys?” The specific version: “Two reasons. The foundation program : I'd rather start with structured training than learn production habits by accident. And InfyTQ: I've used the platform, and a company that builds free learning tools for students takes fresher development seriously.” If you actually used InfyTQ, this answer is unbeatable, because it's evidence.
  • Relocation. Development centres include Bangalore, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Mysuru, Chandigarh, Bhubaneswar and more; training location and posting location can differ. Same rule as everywhere: your spoken answer must match your application form.

What actually rejects people at Infosys

  • Textbook OOP answers with no example. The follow-up “show me that in your project” is where memorised definitions die.
  • Contradicting the application form on relocation, gaps or backlogs, the classic avoidable rejection everywhere in mass hiring.
  • Treating the HR minutes casually because the technical minutes went well. It's one score, not two.
  • Panic freezes. Ask for ten seconds, structure, answer. The pause habit is trainable: how to stop blanking out.

Language: Infosys interviews lean slightly more English-forward than TCS. Verbal communication is explicitly part of what the single round scores. If the interviewer moves into Hindi, follow naturally, keep technical terms in English. The Hinglish answer guide shows the balance.
