TCS interview questions for freshers: NQT to HR, with the answers

TCS hires more freshers than any company in India, which means the process is standardised, and a standardised process can be prepared for precisely. Here is each stage, what it filters, and the questions that actually come up.

Updated 2 September 2026 · 9 min read

The process at a glance

StageWhat it isWhat it filters
NQT (National Qualifier Test)Online test: aptitude, reasoning, verbal, and coding. Your score also decides which package tier you can interview for.Most of the funnel: this is the real gate.
Interview (TR + MR + HR)Usually one sitting, often one video call, with technical, managerial and HR interviewers together. 30–45 minutes.Fundamentals, communication, consistency with your form.
Documents & offerEverything uploads through the NextStep portal; the offer states your tier, base location and the service agreement.Nothing, but messy documents stall offers for weeks.

Timing: TCS runs NQT in cycles, not one annual date. Recent years: a mid-year cycle (register around May–June, interviews July–September) and a second cycle (register around December–February, interviews February–April). If you are reading this in campus season, the interviews happening right now came from the mid-year cycle, and the next registration window is closer than it feels. Check NextStep for your batch's dates.

Ninja, Digital, Prime: know which interview you're in

The three tiers are different interviews wearing the same logo, so find out which one you're sitting for before you prepare:

TierRecent advertised package*Interview difficulty
Ninja₹3.3–3.6 LPABasics of one language, your projects, SQL fundamentals, HR logistics. Depth over breadth is enough.
Digitalaround ₹7 LPAReal data structures & algorithms, deeper project grilling, sometimes system basics. Ninja-level prep will not survive it.
Prime₹9–11.5 LPACloser to a product-company interview: DSA, design thinking, strong CS fundamentals.

*Slabs from recent drives; the binding number is the one in your batch's offer letter. Fresher slabs are fixed: do not negotiate them in the interview.

Ninja candidates can usually attempt an upgrade test for Digital later; mention it only if asked about ambitions; volunteering “I'm really aiming for Digital” in a Ninja interview reads as “I'll be dissatisfied from day one.”

The technical questions TCS actually asks

Panels rotate, but the pattern is stable. For Ninja, expect most of the list below; for Digital/Prime, the same list plus live problem-solving.

  • “Walk me through your final-year project.” The single most reliable question in the whole process. They will pick one line of your explanation and drill: why this database, what breaks if two users submit together, what did you personally build? If you can't answer the third one, the project counts against you, not for you.
  • “Which language are you comfortable in?”, then fundamentals in that language, so pick honestly. Java: OOP pillars with real examples, == vs .equals(), collections. Python: lists vs tuples, dictionaries, what a decorator is. C: pointers, memory.
  • SQL, almost always. Write a query with a JOIN; difference between WHERE and HAVING; what a primary key vs foreign key is. Services companies live on databases; a fresher who is smooth in SQL stands out immediately.
  • One definition ladder. What is an operating system → what is a deadlock → how do you prevent it. Or: what is HTTP → difference between GET and POST. They are checking whether your degree left residue, not whether you can lecture.
  • For Digital/Prime: a live coding prompt: string reversal without built-ins, second-largest element, a simple recursion, where they watch how you think aloud more than whether the syntax is perfect.

The managerial (MR) questions

The MR voice on the panel is estimating one thing: can this person be put in front of a client team without supervision?

  • “Team conflict mein kya karoge?”: give one real incident, small is fine: “Group project mein ek member deadlines miss kar raha tha. Maine pehle directly baat ki, problem unke laptop ki thi, college lab se kaam karva ke humne submission time pe kiya.” Structure: noticed → talked directly → solved. No villains.
  • “Are you okay working on a technology you didn't choose?” This is the real shape of a services career, so the honest yes wins: “Yes, at this stage I care more about learning to build production systems than about which stack. Preference toh hai, condition nahi.”
  • Pressure checks: “What if you're put on support work?”, “What if your rating is average in year one?” Calm, specific, no speech.

The HR questions, TCS edition

The generic five (relocation, shifts, gaps, “why TCS”, other offers) are covered with full scripts in the mass-recruiter HR round guide. What is specifically TCS about this round:

  • The service agreement. Recent batches: one year, with an indemnity amount (around ₹50,000) if you leave early; read your batch's exact terms in the offer. The answer that clears it is two sentences: you've read it, you're comfortable, you're planning to stay. Do not negotiate it.
  • Relocation is real, not theoretical. TCS allocates across its delivery centres (Chennai, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi NCR, Kochi, Indore and more) and your base location can be none of your preferences. The portal asked you this months ago; your spoken answer must match what you ticked. That consistency check is the #1 avoidable rejection in the whole round.
  • “Why TCS?” One level more specific than “brand name”: “Do reasons. Structured training: ITP jaisa foundation program ek startup fresher ko nahi de sakta. Aur project variety: pehle do saal mein teen domains dekhna is better than locking into one product before I know what I like.” Both are true advantages of TCS, so it doesn't sound like flattery.

What actually rejects people at TCS

  • Answers that contradict the NextStep form: relocation, backlog count, gap dates. HR has the form open while you speak. Re-read your entries the night before.
  • A project you can't defend. One borrowed project explained badly outweighs three modest ones you actually built.
  • Trying to negotiate the slab or the agreement. Signals you won't join; the package is fixed either way.
  • Panic silence. A wrong answer recovered calmly scores better than a freeze. If you need a moment: “Give me ten seconds to structure this”, then answer. The habit of pausing is trainable: how to stop blanking out.

And one thing that does not reject people: imperfect English. TCS panels are bilingual in practice. If the interviewer drifts into Hindi, answer in natural Hinglish and keep the technical terms in English, here is how to do that without sounding casual.
