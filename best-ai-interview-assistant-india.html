Tool by tool

Interview Sarthi — best for candidates in India

Ours, so apply salt — but the differences are structural, not marketing. It's the only tool here priced in rupees with UPI (₹399–1,999 one-time, nothing auto-renews), the only one that answers in natural Hinglish when the interviewer switches, and the only one with no vendor cloud: audio goes from your PC straight to Google under your own free Gemini key. Unlimited sessions and hidden-from-share on every plan including the free trial. The honest weaknesses: Windows only, and it's a young product from a solo builder — no mock-interview suite, no resume builder. Pick it if: you interview from a Windows machine and want the lowest total cost with no meters.

Cluely — best-known brand, priciest invisibility

The most famous name in the category, well-funded, polished, Mac support. The catch for interview use: the feature that matters — being invisible when you share your screen — sits in the $149.99/month tier (₹13,000; $44.99/mo if you commit annually). The $19.99 plan is visible in shares, which makes it a meeting copilot, not an interview one. Pick it if: you're on a Mac and want a general-purpose meeting copilot too. Full breakdown: Sarthi vs Cluely.

Final Round AI — best practice platform, harsh live pricing

Strong mock-interview suite, question banks, resume tools, recordings. But the live copilot's month-to-month reality is $149 for 5 sessions — a real interview loop burns through that in one busy week. The advertised $41.67/month requires $500 upfront for a year. Pick it if: you want structured practice more than live help, and interview year-round. Full breakdown: Sarthi vs Final Round AI.

Parakeet AI — best for Mac users who interview rarely

Browser-based, works everywhere, credits never expire — genuinely good for the person who interviews twice a year. The shape of the pricing is the issue for an active job switch: time is metered by the hour (~₹2,600 for 3 hours), and interviews don't respect meters. Pick it if: you're on Mac/Linux, or interview very occasionally. Full breakdown: Sarthi vs Parakeet.

LockedIn AI — broad career copilot

Positions itself as an all-round career assistant (live copilot, coding copilot, resume tools) at $49.99/month, dropping to $29.99/month paid quarterly. Reasonable middle-ground pricing by US standards — still ₹2,600–4,400/month recurring in dollars, English-first, through the vendor's cloud. Pick it if: you want one subscription covering coding assessments plus interviews and don't need Hinglish.

Verve AI — cheapest US-tool entry point

The most affordable of the American tools: free plan with 3 sessions, $38.25/month Standard (5 sessions, 60-minute cap per session), unlimited at ~$59.50/month — and stealth mode is included on all paid plans, which is more honest packaging than most. Limits to know: session caps on the cheaper plans and a 60–90-minute ceiling per call. Pick it if: you want a US tool with stealth without Cluely's invisibility surcharge.

How to actually choose

Start from your machine. Mac → Parakeet, Verve or Cluely. Windows → all six work; Sarthi's price advantage applies fully.

Count your realistic sessions. A two-company loop is 6–10 calls. Any tool with a 5-session cap needs the maths done before the week starts.

Check the invisibility fine print. "Stealth mode" ranges from included-everywhere (Sarthi, Verve paid) to a ₹13,000/month surcharge (Cluely). This is the feature the whole category exists for — never assume it's in the plan you're buying.

Then run the free trials in a mock call. Every tool here has one. One 15-minute mock with a friend on the platform you'll actually use tells you more than every comparison page on the internet, including this one.
