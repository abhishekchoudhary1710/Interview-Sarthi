10 HR interview questions, answered in natural Hinglish

HR rounds in India run bilingual — the interviewer relaxes into Hindi, and a stiff memorised English paragraph suddenly sounds wrong. These are the ten questions that appear in nearly every HR round, each with a Hinglish sample answer and the reasoning, because the reasoning is what transfers to your own story. Adapt, don't memorise.

1. "Tell me about yourself" / "Apne baare mein bataiye"

"Main ek frontend developer hoon, 3 saal ka experience — mostly React mein. Abhi [company] mein product dashboard handle karta hoon jo 40k users use karte hain. Mujhe UI performance ka kaam sabse zyada pasand hai — pichle quarter humne load time 3 second se 1.2 pe laaya. Ab main aisi team dhoondh raha hoon jahan scale bada ho aur ownership zyada mile."

Why it works: present → proof (numbers) → what you want next. Thirty seconds, no childhood, no "myself Rahul from…". The structure survives even if your mind half-blanks.

2. "Why should we hire you?" / "Hum aapko kyun lein?"

"Kyunki jo role mein likha hai — React, performance, thoda backend exposure — wo main abhi daily kar raha hoon, sirf chhote scale pe. Aapko koi aisa chahiye jo pehle mahine se deliver kare, aur mera track record wahi dikhata hai: har jagah maine 90 din ke andar independently ship kiya hai."

Why it works: it maps their job description to your evidence. Never answer this with adjectives ("hardworking, sincere") — answer it with a delivery record.

3. "What are your salary expectations?" / "Salary expectation kya hai?"

"Current CTC 8 LPA hai. Market range is role ke liye 11–14 dekh raha hoon, aur main us range mein kahin bhi comfortable hoon agar role aur growth sahi ho. Aapka budget kya hai is position ke liye?"

Why it works: states a researched range (not a single number), anchors slightly high, and returns the question. Never say "as per company norms" — it reads as unprepared and costs real money.

4. "Why are you leaving your current job?" / "Current company kyun chhod rahe ho?"

"Team achhi hai, seekha bhi bahut — lekin ab wahan growth flat ho gayi hai. Jo naya kaam mujhe karna hai — bade scale pe system design — uska scope wahan limited hai. Isliye move soch-samajh kar hai, frustration mein nahi."

Why it works: zero badmouthing (instant red flag), frames the move as running towards something. Even if you're leaving a mess, this is the version to say.

5. "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" / "5 saal baad khud ko kahan dekhte ho?"

"Honestly, exact designation predict nahi karunga. Par direction clear hai — technical depth pehle, phir team lead karna. 5 saal mein main chahta hoon ki kisi core system ka owner banu aur 2–3 juniors ko mentor kar raha hoon."

Why it works: honest about uncertainty, specific about direction. HR isn't testing prophecy; they're testing whether you'll stay and grow.

6. "Explain the gap in your resume" / "Ye gap kyun hai?"

"2023 mein 8 mahine ka gap hai — family health situation thi, main hi primary caretaker tha. Us dauran main touch mein rehne ke liye freelance projects karta raha — do chhote client sites banayi. Situation ab fully sorted hai, aur pichhle ek saal ka full-time record aap dekh sakte hain."

Why it works: reason (one line, no oversharing) → what you did anyway → closed with proof it's over. Confidence about the gap matters more than the gap.

7. "What is your biggest weakness?" / "Apni kami batao"

"Main kaam delegate karne mein slow hoon — lagta hai khud karunga toh jaldi hoga. Pichhle saal isse sprint mein bottleneck bana, toh ab maine rule banaya hai: jo task koi aur 80% kar sakta hai, wo main de deta hoon aur sirf review karta hoon. Abhi bhi perfect nahi hoon ispe, par pehle se kaafi behtar."

Why it works: a real weakness (not "I work too hard"), plus the system you built around it. The fix is the answer; the weakness is just the setup.

8. "Why this company?" / "Humari company hi kyun?"

"Do reasons. Ek — aapka product main khud use karta hoon, aur mujhe pata hai onboarding flow kahan chhoot-ta hai; us pe kaam karna exciting lagta hai. Do — aapke engineering blog se lagta hai yahan log likhte-sochte hain, sirf tickets close nahi karte. Waisi team mein main tez seekhta hoon."

Why it works: proves 20 minutes of research (product + blog). This question is a research test wearing a loyalty costume — generic praise fails it.

9. "Are you interviewing elsewhere?" / "Aur kahin process chal raha hai?"

"Haan, do aur companies ke saath process chal raha hai — same tarah ke roles. Par main timeline transparent rakhunga, aur agar yahan fit banta hai toh ye role meri priority list mein upar hai, kyunki [specific reason]."

Why it works: honesty (they assume you are anyway) + a genuine reason they're preferred. Creates healthy urgency without games.

10. "Do you have any questions for us?" / "Aapke koi sawaal?"

"Haan, do sawaal. Pehla — is role mein 90 din baad success kaisa dikhta hai aapki nazar mein? Aur doosra — team ka pichhla sabse mushkil quarter kaunsa tha aur kyun?"

Why it works: "no questions" reads as no interest. These two work in every company, and the second one often gets you the most honest information of the whole day.

The pattern across all ten: structure + specifics + zero negativity. Hinglish is not a downgrade from English — delivered with numbers and calm, it reads as confident and real. Match the interviewer's language, keep technical words in English, and never memorise sentences — memorise the beats.
