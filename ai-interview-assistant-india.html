A plain guide for candidates — what these tools actually do during a live interview, what the popular ones cost in rupees, and the five things to check before paying for any of them. Updated August 2026.

What an AI interview assistant does

During an online interview on Teams, Zoom or Google Meet, the tool listens to the call, transcribes the interviewer's question in real time, and shows you a suggested answer within a couple of seconds — on an overlay only you can see. Good ones ground the answer in your resume, so "walk me through your last project" is answered with your actual project, not a template. Think of it as the safety net for the moment your mind goes blank — the knowledge was always yours; the tool just brings it back under pressure.

Five things to check before buying any of them

1. Is invisibility included or extra? Being hidden from screen share is the whole point in an interview. On some tools it's the top-priced tier (Cluely: $149.99/month). On Interview Sarthi every pass and the free trial include it.

2. Does it join the call as a bot? Some tools add a "notetaker" participant to the meeting — visible to the interviewer, and blockable by the host. For interviews you want a desktop overlay that touches nothing in the meeting. No bot ever joins with Interview Sarthi.

3. Does it understand the way Indian interviews actually run? Interviewers here switch mid-sentence between English and Hindi. A tool that only transcribes English will garble half the conversation. Interview Sarthi follows the interviewer and answers in English or natural Hinglish.

4. Can you pay without an international card? Dollar subscriptions need a card enabled for international recurring payments — a real blocker for many candidates. Interview Sarthi checkout is UPI or any Indian card, in rupees, one-time.

5. Where does your audio go? Your interview is sensitive. Check whether the vendor's servers sit between you and the AI. Interview Sarthi runs entirely on your PC and streams audio directly to Google under your own key — the seller never receives your audio, resume or transcripts.

Is using one allowed?

That depends on the interview, the employer and your institution — some organisations prohibit assistance tools, and some interviews are proctored.
