Interview Sarthi vs Cluely: the comparison in rupees

Both put AI answers on your screen during a live call. The differences that matter are what invisibility costs, what you pay per interview season, and whether the answers sound like you. Updated August 2026.

The short version

Cluely is a polished, VC-funded meeting copilot at $19.99/month — but hiding it from screen share costs $149.99/month (about ₹13,000). Interview Sarthi is built for one job — online interviews in India — costs ₹399–1,999 one-time, and hidden-from-share is included in every pass and the free trial.

What the price difference actually means

A typical job switch in India takes 4–10 weeks of interviewing. On Cluely, doing that invisibly costs $149.99 × 2–3 months ≈ ₹26,000–39,000, billed to an international card. On Interview Sarthi, the 90-day pass is ₹1,999 once — about the cost of one hour with an interview coach — and it stops charging you the moment it ends, because there is nothing to cancel.

Where Cluely is genuinely better

Honesty helps you choose: Cluely runs on Mac (Sarthi is Windows-only), it is a general meeting copilot with notetaking for daily standups and sales calls, and it has a larger team behind it. If you are on a MacBook, or you want an everyday meeting notetaker rather than an interview tool, Cluely is the right pick.

Where Interview Sarthi is built differently

Sarthi does one thing: live online interviews, for candidates in India. The answers speak as you — grounded in your own resume and projects, so "tell me about your Redis project" gets your Redis project, not a generic essay. It follows the interviewer between English and Hinglish. No bot joins your meeting, nothing is installed server-side, and your audio goes only to Google under your own free key — we never see it.

FAQ

Is Interview Sarthi cheaper than Cluely?
Yes — every pass is one-time. ₹1,999 covers 90 days; Cluely's invisibility tier costs about ₹13,000 per month.

Does the free trial hide from screen share too?
Yes. The trial (3 sessions of 15 minutes) is the full product, including hidden-from-capture.

Can my interviewer detect it?
The overlay is excluded from screen capture on Windows, so Teams, Zoom, Meet and OBS shares don't show it. A phone camera pointed at your screen, or certain proctoring tools, are different — the same limits apply to every tool in this category, whatever their marketing says. Use it within the rules that apply to your interview.
