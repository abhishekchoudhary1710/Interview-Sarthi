How it works

Log your day. Take the call. Confirm the recap.

01 · One minute a day

Log what happened

Say it or type it: "finished the webhook check, waiting on Ravi for sandbox keys, tomorrow I start the dashboard". The junior splits it into done, doing, blocked and next. You confirm each line, and only confirmed lines are ever used.

02 · During the call

Start the meeting panel

It hears only the other side of the call; your microphone is never sent. When the client asks you something, the reply appears in a second or two, with the log entry it came from. Missed one? Press help me answer.

03 · After the call

Confirm the recap

Status changes, decisions, action items and commitments arrive as drafts, with a follow-up message you can copy. Tick what is true and it joins the log, ready for the next call.

What it will not do

A junior, not a guesser.

It never invents progress.

A reply may only state a date, name, number or commitment that is in an entry you confirmed, and it shows which one. With nothing to cite it suggests "I'll confirm that after the call and send it across today."

It never joins the meeting.

No bot, no invite, no extra participant. It listens to the sound your laptop plays, so it works on calls the client hosts, in Teams, Zoom, Meet, Webex or anything else.

It never sends anything.

Recaps, follow-up messages and log lines are drafts until you confirm them. The Git, GitHub and Jira connections are read-only.

It never sees your projects on our servers.

The log is one file on your laptop. Audio and the project brief go to Google Gemini on your own free key, and nothing else leaves. We see licence checks and the problem reports you choose to send.

Questions people ask

Short answers.

What does it cost?

Projects, the daily log, voice notes, the Git, GitHub and Jira feeders and the Ask junior are free with no limit. Live replies during a call are free for the first 30 minutes on a computer; after that a Month Pass is Rs 99 for 30 days on two computers, paid once by UPI, card or netbanking, never renewing by itself.

Does a bot join my meeting?

No. Nothing joins the call and nobody is invited. The app listens to the sound your computer plays, so it works with Teams, Zoom, Google Meet, Webex and any other call app, including calls the client hosts.

Where does the audio go?

From your laptop to Google Gemini on your own free API key. Your microphone is never sent; only the other side of the call is. Nothing about your projects reaches our servers: the log lives in one file on your laptop, and the only things we see are licence checks and the problem reports you choose to send. Google's free tier may use data to improve its models, so check your workplace's AI policy before using it on confidential calls.

Can it invent a status?

It is built not to. A reply may only state a date, name, number or commitment that is in an entry you confirmed, and the panel shows which entry it came from. If your log has nothing on the question, it suggests a polite deferral instead of guessing.

Does the client see the panel?

On Windows the panel is excluded from screen sharing in the apps that support it, like a note on your own desk. Share one window rather than the whole screen when you are unsure, and use it only where your workplace allows AI tools.

Hinglish?

Replies are in plain English, because that is what you will say. Optionally, each client question gets a one-line Hinglish explanation under it, and voice notes can be spoken in Hinglish.

Does it connect to Jira, GitHub, Slack or Teams?

Today: a local git folder with no token, GitHub by personal token, and Jira Cloud by email and API token. All read-only, none needs an admin. Slack, Teams chat and Gmail are planned and need your workspace's approval, so they come later.

How is this different from Interview Sarthi?

Interview Sarthi helps a candidate during a job interview, from their CV. MeetingSarthi helps an employee during a client call at work, from their own project log. Same engine, separate apps, separate passes: a key for one does not open the other.
