What AI mock interviews cost in India

We opened the pricing page of 28 tools, from an Indian connection, and wrote down what each one charges. Where a company shows rupees to Indian visitors, that is the number below. Nothing here is estimated.

The short answer. Practising with AI costs between nothing and about Rs 3,000 a month in India. The floor is Rs 9 for a single session. Free tiers are small, usually one to five sessions or 15 to 30 minutes, once. A mock interview with a real person costs Rs 499 to Rs 1,799 an hour, roughly fifty times more than the AI ones.

Five things the prices tell you

1. Almost every paid voice plan caps how much you practise

Greenroom caps at 10 or 30 mocks a month. Yoodli caps at 10 roleplays a week. Tough Tongue and Himalayas sell minutes outright and charge extra beyond them. The reason is simple: a spoken interview costs the company money for every minute it runs.

2. The exception is tools that use your own AI key

When you bring your own free Google Gemini key, the company pays nothing per minute, so it has no reason to ration your practice. Tough Tongue offers this as an extra on its $20 plan, charging one cent a minute on top. Prep Sarthi is built that way by default, which is why its passes are unlimited inside the window.

3. Indian buyers are being sold subscriptions they do not want

HireStepX leads with "no subscription, no monthly guilt". CrackInterviewAI says its pass is "prepaid and never auto-renews". Interview Sidekick's pricing page has to explain that the Rs 15,000 figure your bank app shows is a UPI AutoPay authorisation ceiling and not a charge. One-time passes avoid that whole conversation.

4. Free tiers are small everywhere

One to five sessions, or 15 to 30 minutes, usually once and never renewed. Final Round AI gives no free live use at all. If a tool offers a generous free tier, check whether it is text rather than voice, because text costs a fraction of what speech does.

5. A human mock costs about fifty times an AI one

Rs 499 to Rs 1,799 an hour in India, and $179 upwards abroad. That is not an argument against human mocks. It is an argument for doing ten AI rounds first, so you do not spend a paid hour with a person discovering that you ramble.

How to choose, in one paragraph

If you want to try the idea, start with something free and see whether talking to a machine helps you at all. If you have one interview this week, a short pass costs less than lunch and you will use it hard for a few days. If you are in placement season and will interview repeatedly for a month, compare the monthly plans on their caps, not their prices: Rs 499 for 10 mocks is worse value than Rs 249 for as many as you want. And if a tool asks for your own Google key, that is usually why it can afford not to count your minutes.
